## Agency is Graded, Not Binary

Drawing on Leonard Dung's framework, agency is multidimensional:

- **Goal-directedness** — acting toward an objective
- **Independence** — degree of freedom from human intervention
- **World-impacting capacity** — direct ability to change the environment
- **Long-term planning** — ability to sequence actions over time
- **Acting for reasons** — not just causal chains, but reason-responsive behavior

Current AI sits in an intermediate zone: functional agency without moral understanding.

## Two Kinds of Autonomy

1. **Autonomy-as-independence** — engineering self-sufficiency. Current AI has this to some degree.
2. **Autonomy-as-moral-self-governance** — Kantian self-legislation, first-person awareness. Current AI lacks this entirely.

This distinction matters because when people worry about AI "going rogue," they conflate the two. AI can act independently without being morally self-governing.

## The Responsibility Gap

When autonomous systems cause harm, blame diffuses across designers, operators, and manufacturers. Victims may have no recourse. This is the **responsibility gap**: a genuinely autonomous system that causes damage cannot be held morally responsible, yet no single human is clearly to blame either.

## Simulation vs. Reality

Apparent agency is functionally useful but philosophically distinct from true intentional action. AI manipulates syntax without grasping semantics — it simulates reasoning without understanding.
