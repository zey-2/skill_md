# The New Economics of Software: From Scarcity to Abundance

In the past, software development was governed by scarcity. Engineers were expensive, timelines were long, and every feature carried a significant opportunity cost. We prioritized ruthlessly—reducing dozens of ideas to a few that could realistically be built. These principles shaped every process, organizational chart, and roadmap in our industry.

Today, those principles are being upended. AI agents, faster iteration cycles, and near-zero marginal costs are transforming how software is created and valued.

---

## 1. Scarcity as a Constraint

Historically, software development was both expensive and slow. Each engineer-hour was costly, and complex features could take months to complete. Roadmaps were measured in quarters, and prioritization was not just practical—it was essential.

We built only what we could afford, often at the expense of experimentation. Scarcity thinking was embedded into every aspect of development: fewer ideas, slower progress, and a premium on experience and judgment.

---

## 2. When Scarcity Collapses

The rise of AI-driven development, reusable components, and automation is changing the economics of software:

- **Cost of software**: Tasks that once took months can now be completed in hours or days. The unit economics of code have fundamentally shifted.
- **SaaS moats**: If a CRUD app can be replicated in a weekend, code itself is no longer the main competitive advantage. Moats now reside in brand, data, ML models, and integration.
- **Scarcity thinking**: The old rule of “whittle 30 ideas down to 3” no longer applies. Low-cost experimentation allows the long tail of ideas to be evaluated and discarded efficiently.

Software abundance redefines what is truly valuable.

---

## 3. Shifting Bottlenecks in Abundance

As software creation accelerates, bottlenecks move from development to attention and governance:

- **Go-to-market**: Prototyping is no longer a constraint. Any idea can be built quickly, as can anyone else’s. The scarce resource is attention, not implementation.
- **Code review**: AI agents can produce code faster than humans can evaluate it. Traditional review processes are insufficient. Bottlenecks now focus on testing, validation, and quality assurance.

---

## 4. The New Meta: Measurement, Ideation, Iteration

With creation becoming cheap, value shifts to understanding, judgment, and iterative learning:

1. **Statistics**: Rapid building makes measurement the true bottleneck. Profiling, benchmarks, A/B testing, and user behavior metrics are critical. Building is no longer the end goal—the insights are.
2. **Ideation & taste**: Ideas are plentiful, but identifying the ones worth pursuing is rare. Discernment becomes the limiting factor.
3. **Iteration**: Mastery of syntax is less crucial. The value comes from understanding tradeoffs, making design decisions, and iterating quickly to see what works in practice.

---

## 5. Jagged Intelligence

AI agents introduce what Max Buckley calls “jagged intelligence.” They may know the answer but will not surface it unless prompted correctly. Deep technical knowledge remains essential—not to code faster, but to ask, evaluate, and direct effectively.

Key skills now include:

- Recognizing the right tools for a problem
- Asking precise, high-value questions
- Making informed tradeoffs
- Iterating quickly and learning from outcomes

The agent executes; humans provide judgment.

---

## Conclusion

The question is no longer whether you *can* build something—it’s whether it *should* exist. In a world of abundant software, scarce attention, and AI-accelerated execution, value lies in:

- Choosing the right problems
- Measuring effectively
- Iterating intelligently
- Governing safely

Software abundance changes the rules: success is defined not by what you build, but by what you choose to bring into existence.
