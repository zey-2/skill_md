# The Ideation Process from Problems to Practical Solutions

**Source**: ChatGPT-generated guide
**Author**: (none specified)
**Published**: (none specified)

## Summary

A structured guide to the ideation process covering the full cycle from defining challenges through research, divergent generation, evaluation, prototyping, and learning. The process follows: **Understand → Explore → Generate → Combine → Evaluate → Prototype → Learn**. It emphasizes diverging widely before converging carefully, and treats stopping early as a valid outcome rather than failure.

## Key Points

### The Seven-Stage Pipeline

The ideation process is not brainstorming — it is a disciplined pipeline:

1. **Define the Challenge** — A clear problem statement identifies the target user, their problem, the desired outcome, and practical constraints. "A poorly defined problem can lead to an impressive solution that addresses the wrong need."
2. **Gather Insights** — User interviews, observation, surveys, competitor analysis, workflow reviews. The goal is to uncover patterns, frustrations, and unmet needs — not just collect information.
3. **Reframe the Problem** — "How might we" questions direct attention to different parts of the problem. The way a problem is described influences the types of solutions generated.
4. **Generate a Wide Range of Ideas** — Divergent stage. Quantity before quality. Methods: brainwriting, mind mapping, SCAMPER, Crazy 8s, Worst Possible Idea, analogy thinking.
5. **Group and Combine Ideas** — Organize into themes, find duplication and complementary features. "The strongest concept is often not a single original idea."
6. **Evaluate and Prioritise** — Convergent stage. Criteria: user value, feasibility, cost, time, strategic relevance, risk, differentiation. Impact-effort matrix.
7. **Develop into a Concept** — Describe who, what, how, benefit, assumptions, risks, and differentiation.

### The Iteration Loop

Ideation is iterative, not linear: **Problem → Ideas → Prototype → Evidence → Improved understanding → Better ideas**. Testing reveals new information about the original problem, leading to better questions.

### Prototyping as Assumption Testing

A prototype is a simplified representation whose purpose is to test whether the idea is understandable, useful, and workable — not to create a finished product. Options range from paper sketches to simple Python scripts to manually operated services. "This approach reduces cost and allows weaknesses to be discovered early."

### Refine, Pivot, Expand, or Stop

After testing: refine if the concept works but needs improvement, pivot if the problem is valid but the solution is unsuitable, expand if testing reveals a larger opportunity, stop if the idea doesn't provide enough value. "Stopping an idea is not necessarily a failure."
