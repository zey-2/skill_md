# Making AI Work: Leadership, Lab, and Crowd

**Source**: One Useful Thing, `https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/making-ai-work-leadership-lab-and`
**Author**: [[Ethan Mollick]]
**Published**: 2025-05-22

## Summary

Mollick argues that individual AI productivity gains do not naturally translate to organizational performance. The gap exists because companies have atrophied the "muscles for organizational innovation" — decades of outsourcing change to consultants and enterprise software vendors leave them unprepared for a transformation where nobody has a playbook. The framework for closing the gap is Leadership (vision and incentives), Lab (centralized experimentation), and Crowd (decentralized worker discovery).

## Key Points

### The Four Facts

1. **AI boosts work performance.** Danish study: users say AI halved working time for 41% of tasks. American survey: workers report tripling productivity (90 min → 30 min). Controlled experiments confirm gains in product development, sales, consulting, coding, law, and call centers.
2. **Large percentage using AI at work.** Danish study: 65% of marketers, 64% of journalists, 30% of lawyers. American workers: 30% in Dec 2024 → 40% in Apr 2025. ChatGPT is the 4th most visited website.
3. **More transformational gains available than most realize.** Deep research, agents, and increasingly smart systems can produce high-quality outcomes.
4. **Companies are not capturing these gains.** Companies report small to moderate gains; no major impact on wages or hours worked as of end of 2024.

The reconciliation: individual performance gains do not naturally translate to organizational performance. Organizational innovation is required — rethinking incentives, processes, and the nature of work.

### Leadership

- Urgency alone is insufficient. Shopify and Duolingo CEO memos signal "why now" but stop short of painting what the AI-powered future *actually looks and feels like*.
- Workers are not motivated by performance gains or bottom lines. They want clear, vivid images of the future: what will work be like? Will efficiency gains lead to layoffs or growth? How will AI use be rewarded or punished?
- Leaders need to anticipate how work changes at the task level. AI replaces specific tasks within jobs, not entire jobs.
- Dispersing software engineering teams into cross-functional groups with subject matter experts allows "vibework" — building in days what used to take months of cross-departmental coordination.

### The Crowd

- Innovation and performance improvements happen in experienced workers who figure out how to use AI for their own work through trial and error.
- Official AI chatbot adoption maxes out at ~20% of workers, yet 40%+ admit using AI at work with large reported gains. The gap = **Secret Cyborgs** hiding their AI use.
- Reasons workers hide AI use: fear of punishment, desire for credit, fear of layoffs, expectation of more work, no reward for revealing.
- Solutions: clear experimentation zones, hands-on training over prompting techniques, reassurance against layoffs, massive incentives (vacations, promotions, cash), leadership modeling.

### The Lab

- An ambidextrous team engaging in both exploration (future capabilities) and exploitation (shipping products now).
- Consists of subject matter experts — a mix of technologists and non-technologists. The Crowd provides the researchers: enthusiasts who discover and share AI workflows.
- What the Lab builds:
  - Take Crowd-discovered prompts/solutions and distribute them widely, fast.
  - Build organization-specific AI benchmarks (not generic math/coding benchmarks). "Vibes-based" benchmarks are fine.
  - Build things that don't work yet — deployable prototypes that improve automatically as models improve.
  - Build provocations: visceral demos that jolt people into understanding AI potential.

### Re-examining the Organization

- When research takes minutes instead of weeks, the bottleneck is figuring out what research to do. When code is fast, the limitation is knowing what to build. When content is instant, the constraint is knowing what matters.
- Treating AI adoption as an organizational learning challenge, not a technical one.
- Building feedback loops between Leadership, Lab, and Crowd that let the company learn faster than competitors.

## Evidence

- Danish study (BFI Chicago): representative sample of knowledge workers on AI time savings.
- American survey (SSRN 5136877): 30% → 40% adoption in 4 months.
- Controlled experiments: product development, sales, consulting, coding (SSRN 4945566), law students (SSRN 5162111), call centers (NBER 31161).
- NBER 33777: no major impact on wages or hours worked as of end of 2024.
- Shopify CEO memo (Tobi Lütke) and Duolingo CEO memo as viral leadership signals.
- Andrew Carton (Wharton): workers motivated by vivid future images, not performance metrics.
- Manus agent demo: 45-page business model analysis, PowerPoint, website, financial deep dive from a short prompt.
- Veo 3 video generation: text-to-video with sound for advertising.
- General Counsel's office as AI adoption choke point in regulated industries.

## Contradictions or Tensions

- Mollick says "nobody has special information" about how to best use AI at your company, yet the Lab pattern suggests centralized expertise is still needed. The tension: centralized knowledge accelerates adoption, but the discoveries must come from the Crowd.
- The "Secret Cyborgs" finding (20% official adoption, 40% actual use) tensions with the enterprise flywheel's "public sharing" stage. If workers hide their use, the flywheel stalls at the early-wins stage.
- Mollick's 2025 article predates the agent era (Claude Code, Codex, OpenClaw). The Lab's "build things that don't work yet" advice has aged well — it predicted the rapid improvement cycle that made agents viable within months.
- The claim that "nobody has a playbook" tensions with the growing body of case studies in this wiki (Ramp, Block, Every, Anthropic). The playbook is emerging, but it's still company-specific.

## Open Questions

- How does the Leadership/Lab/Crowd framework scale below 100 people? Small companies may not need a formal Lab.
- What is the minimum viable Lab? One person? Three? A rotating committee?
- How do you measure whether the Lab is producing organizational value vs. just interesting demos?
- The 20% → 40% adoption gap was measured in Dec 2024–Apr 2025. Has it widened or narrowed since?
