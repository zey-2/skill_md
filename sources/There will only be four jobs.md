# There Will Only Be Four Jobs

**Source**: Substack, `https://99d.substack.com/p/there-will-only-be-four-jobs`
**Author**: Yoni Rechtman
**Published**: 2026-04-03
**Created**: 2026-05-11

## Summary

Yoni Rechtman argues that AI-native companies are reorganizing work around styles of production rather than traditional job titles. The provocative "four jobs" are not literal roles but archetypes: high-velocity product-minded builders, stabilizers who make systems robust, judgment-heavy adults who govern acceleration, and interface people who make the company legible and attractive.

## Key Points

- The source says these are working styles and archetypes, not formal job descriptions.
- The old product/design/engineering triangle organized around categories of output. In an AI-native company, anyone can produce code, specs, designs, internal tools, or automations, so what people produce becomes less defining than how they produce.
- "Product eng/vibe coder/PM/slop cannon" describes the high-velocity, high-tool-use generalist who is commercial, product-minded, and comfortable shipping across role boundaries.
- "SREs/infra/security/systems" describes stabilizers who stitch together the increased volume of output and make it secure, robust, and maintainable.
- "Adults" describes people with judgment, earned intuition, authority, and risk sense who can slow or redirect acceleration before it goes off course.
- "Hot people" is the author's intentionally provocative label for interface roles: people who make the product or organization easy to understand, attractive, trustworthy, and pleasant to interact with.
- The author is not an employment doomer. The claim is that large companies may become more efficient, but there may also be more companies and blurrier boundaries around "tech."

## Evidence

- The source explicitly frames the four categories as latent traits that cut across job titles and organizations.
- It says the highest-performing companies will have product-engineer/slop-cannon traits across product, engineering, sales, ops, talent, finance, CX, and marketing.
- It generalizes SRE beyond infrastructure: marketing QA and copy editing can serve the same stabilizing function as engineering reliability work.
- It warns that decentralized execution increases the need for adults who can apply judgment and stop catastrophic acceleration.
- It cites the alleged one-person GLP-1 company as an example of what can happen when high-velocity execution lacks adult supervision, while noting the numbers may be fake or misleading.

## Contradictions or Tensions

- The source's language is intentionally memetic and loose. It is useful as a taxonomy of working styles, but not precise enough to replace actual role design, compensation ladders, or hiring rubrics.
- The "slop cannon" category overlaps with agentic engineering but can also become the failure mode it names if high output is not paired with stabilizers and adults.
- The source says these archetypes may replace product/design/engineering, while other sources in the wiki emphasize that product sense, design taste, engineering depth, and security expertise still matter. A reasonable synthesis is that functional skills remain necessary, but role boundaries blur.

## Open Questions

- How should companies translate these archetypes into hiring, promotion, and team-design practices without reducing people to memes?
- Which archetypes are scarce at different company stages?
- How do stabilizer and adult roles gain enough authority when speed and output are culturally rewarded?
