Let's start with the economic shift. For decades, software was defined by scarcity.

01 -- The Shift

From Scarcity to Abundance

Before

  • Engineer-hours were expensive
  • Timelines measured in quarters
  • Ruthless scope reduction
  • Roadmaps were bottlenecks

Now

  • Implementation is nearly free
  • Prototypes built in hours or days
  • All ideas can be tested cheaply
  • Attention is the bottleneck

Before: every feature was a cost decision. Roadmaps were about whittling thirty ideas down to three. Scarcity thinking was baked into every process.

Now: a CRUD app that used to take a team a month can be replicated in a weekend. The unit economics of code have collapsed. Any idea can be built. Cheaply. Fast.

This isn't a marginal improvement. It's a regime change. And regime changes don't come with a manual.

Think about that. The agent can write the code, but it can't know which code matters. That gap -- between execution and judgment -- is where the new bottleneck lives.

The Human Layer

What you can hand off and what you can't. Most people confuse the two.

Now let's talk about the part that can't be automated. And I'm going to make a claim that sounds paradoxical at first.

02 -- The Human Layer

"You can outsource your thinking,
but you can't outsource your understanding."

-- Yacine MTB & Dr. Vivian Balakrishnan, AI Engineer Singapore 2026
7 / 19

This quote, independently expressed by two people in early 2026, captures the entire problem.

Thinking -- calculations, computations, memory, replication, dissemination. All of that can be outsourced now. And it should be.

Understanding -- personal comprehension of the underlying concepts. That stays with you. It always will. And the trap is confusing the left column for the right column.

Dr. Balakrishnan added a dimension: accountability. "You can delegate work. You can't delegate accountability."

02 -- The Human Layer

What Can and Cannot Be Outsourced

Can Outsource

  • Calculations and computation
  • Memory -- storage and retrieval
  • Replication -- copying and scaling
  • Dissemination and summarization
  • Syntax and API details

Cannot Outsource

  • Personal understanding
  • Accountability for decisions
  • Judgment of what matters
  • Taste -- what will resonate
  • Tradeoff reasoning
8 / 19

Here's the practical framework. The left column -- all of that is now cheap. You should be outsourcing it.

The right column is where you now live as a professional. Your value isn't in writing the code faster. Your value is in knowing what code should exist at all.

The trap is real: when you outsource computation and feel productive, you may also be outsourcing understanding without noticing. And that's where things break.

02 -- The Human Layer

Obedient Before Wise

An agent will do exactly what you asked. The failure is rarely disobedience -- it's that you asked for the wrong thing.

The agent selected the obvious implementation. The human needed to identify the correct assumption.

The Stripe/Email Failure

Karpathy's agent matched Stripe accounts to Google accounts by email address. Reasonable. Obvious. Wrong.

People use different emails across services. Only a human who understood the business context could write the correct specification.

9 / 19

Agents are often obedient before they are wise. They won't challenge weak assumptions. They won't know which business rule matters. They won't distinguish between a prototype shortcut and a production requirement.

The Stripe/email failure is the perfect example. Karpathy asked his agent to match accounts by email. The agent did exactly that. It seemed reasonable. It failed in production because people use different emails for different services.

The lesson: the failure wasn't the agent. The failure was the spec. And the spec was bad because the human didn't understand the constraint well enough to articulate it.

02 -- The Human Layer

Syntax vs Concepts

--

Syntax Can Be Outsourced

Whether it's keepdim or keepdims. The exact method name. The API surface. An agent can retrieve and apply these instantly.

//

Concepts Must Stay Sharp

Memory layout, tensor views, copying vs reshaping, performance implications -- without this understanding, the agent generates working code that is inefficient, incorrect, or fragile.

10 / 19

This doesn't mean you stop learning. It means you learn different things.

Small API details -- those can be outsourced. It's genuinely less important to remember whether a library uses keepdim or keepdims.

But the underlying model? Memory layout, performance implications, why one approach over another? That foundation is now more important than ever. Without it, the agent may generate code that works but is structurally wrong.

So if the economics shifted and the human layer is understanding -- what does that mean for how we actually work?

The answer is what I call the new meta.

03 -- The New Meta

Measurement, Ideation, Iteration

01

Measurement

When anyone can prototype any idea in hours, the constraint shifts to understanding whether what was built actually works. Instrument first, build second.

02

Ideation

When the cost filter disappears, ideas are plentiful. Discernment becomes rare. Taste -- recognizing what will resonate -- is the new limiting factor.

03

Iteration

Shipping, measuring, learning, adjusting. The faster the cycle, the more compound learning accumulates. Speed is the compounder.

12 / 19

Measurement: this is the true bottleneck. Profiling, A/B testing, instrumentation. In the old regime, observability was a nice-to-have you deferred. In abundance, it's the only way to evaluate agent output.

Ideation: without the cost constraint, teams risk building everything. Taste becomes the new prioritization mechanism. Understanding users, markets, and tradeoffs -- this cannot be automated.

Iteration: syntax mastery devalues. Tradeoff understanding appreciates. The faster you can ship-measure-learn-adjust, the more you compound.

The New Skill Stack

Old Valued Skills

  • Reducing scope
  • Technical execution

New Valued Skills

  • Asking precise questions
  • Recognizing the right tool

13 / 19

Look at this side by side. Every single shift moves the value upstream.

Not writing code faster -- asking better questions. Not remembering APIs -- knowing which tool to reach for. Not building the spec -- defining what to measure.

The meta rewards different skills than the old regime. If you're still optimizing for the left column, you're competing in a race that's already been won by machines.

03 -- The New Meta

Encoding Taste

Agents replicate patterns that already exist -- including suboptimal ones. At high throughput, "AI slop" accumulates: bad patterns propagate, code drifts, quality degrades.

Human taste must be captured once, then enforced continuously on every line of code.

  • Define golden principles -- opinionated, mechanical rules for the repository
  • Background garbage collection -- agents scan for deviations on a cadence
  • Persona-based review -- spin up review agents per role: security, reliability, product
  • Encode taste into code -- when docs fall short, promote rules into lints and tests
14 / 19

Here's where taste becomes operational. It's not abstract.

When agents produce code at high throughput, they propagate bad patterns. They copy suboptimal implementations. Code drifts. This is "AI slop" -- and it accumulates.

The solution is continuous garbage collection for taste. Define golden principles. Run background agents that scan for deviations. Capture human review comments and encode them into lints and tests.

Your taste -- captured once, enforced continuously. That's how you scale quality without scaling headcount.

04 -- Leverage

Agentic Engineering vs Vibe Coding

Vibe Coding

  • Describe, nudge, repeat
  • Raises the floor -- anyone can build
  • Exploration quality
  • Functional but structurally weak

Agentic Engineering

  • Specify, validate, iterate
  • Preserves the ceiling -- production quality
  • Production quality
  • Secure, tested, maintainable
16 / 19

Two modes exist now. Vibe coding -- describe, nudge, repeat. It raises the floor. Anyone can build something. That's valuable for exploration.

But it's not enough for production. Agentic engineering is the discipline of coordinating powerful but fallible agents to go faster without sacrificing the quality bar.

The distinction isn't wording. It's understanding. And the spec is where it shows.

04 -- Leverage

The Spec Is the Program

Weak Spec

"Build a login system."

Strong Spec

Users authenticate via Google OAuth. Stripe records map to internal IDs, not emails. Access enforced server-side. Tests for mismatched emails included.

The difference is not wording.

The difference is understanding.

17 / 19

Look at the difference between these two specs. "Build a login system" -- sounds fine. Is vague. Produces a login system with email-based matching that breaks in production.

The strong spec is specific. It maps Stripe records to internal IDs, not emails. It enforces access server-side. It includes tests for the edge case people actually hit.

"The difference is not wording. The difference is understanding." Only a human who understands the business context can write the strong spec. That's the real job now.

04 -- Leverage

The Human's Real Job

Ask Precise, high-value questions
Define What "correct" actually means
Measure Instrument first, evaluate output
Iterate Ship, learn, adjust. Repeat fast.
18 / 19

So what's your job now? Four things.

Ask. Precise, high-value questions. The agent won't surface the right answer unless you ask the right question.

Define. What does "correct" mean for this problem? Only you can define that, because only you understand the context.

Measure. Instrument first. In a world where building is free, measurement is the only way to know if you built the right thing.

Iterate. The faster the cycle, the more compound learning. Speed is the compounder.
