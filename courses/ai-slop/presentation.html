What is AI Slop?

When machines make things fast, cheap, and barely good enough — and we all pay the price.

Based on research from the LLM Wiki

Welcome — today I want to talk about something you've probably encountered without knowing its name.

It's called "AI slop." You may have heard the term thrown around online. But what does it actually mean?

It's not just about bad images or weird articles. It's a fundamental problem with how we're building things with AI right now.

I'll walk you through what it is, why it matters, and what we can do about it — in plain language, no tech jargon.

THE CORE IDEA

Slop is what happens when fast beats good

AI tools can produce enormous amounts of content very quickly. But speed comes with a hidden cost: the quality is uneven, and the bad stuff accumulates.

  • AI copies existing patterns — including the bad ones
  • Each piece looks reasonable on its own
  • But together, they create a growing mess
  • Like technical debt, but on steroids

"In today's ever-evolving digital landscape, leveraging cutting-edge AI-powered solutions has become paramount for optimizing your workflow and maximizing synergistic outcomes across all verticals..."

^ This means nothing. It was written to sound smart. It's slop.

2 / 8

Let me define it clearly: AI slop is low-quality output that accumulates because AI tools prioritize speed over craftsmanship.

Think of it like this: if you asked a hundred people to write a paragraph about "digital transformation" as fast as possible, you'd get a lot of words that sound impressive but say nothing. Now imagine a machine doing that a million times per day.

The key insight from the wiki is that AI doesn't just make bad things — it copies patterns that already exist, including the bad ones, and spreads them everywhere.

That specimen on screen? That's the kind of text AI generates by default. It's grammatically correct. It uses the right words. It communicates zero information.

Design and visuals — you know that feeling when every startup website looks identical? That's visual slop. The "taste-skill" project exists specifically to combat this.

One million lines of code produced by AI in five months. Not a single line was written by a human.

The point isn't that AI is bad — it's that at this scale, even a 5% error rate creates thousands of problems.

The Real Cost

It's not the bad output. It's what happens next.

6 / 8

Here's the thing most people miss: the real cost isn't the bad content itself. It's the compounding effect.

The wiki compares it to a high-interest loan. It's almost always better to pay it down continuously than to let it compound.

THE CHOICE

Slop vs. Craftsmanship

AI Slop

  • Fast, cheap, good-enough
  • Copies existing patterns blindly
  • Each piece looks OK alone
  • Accumulates into chaos
  • "In today's digital landscape..."

Craftsmanship

  • Slower, deliberate, opinionated
  • Questions whether patterns are good
  • Works as part of a whole
  • Improves over time
  • Clear, specific, human

"Craftsmanship is what will set you apart from vibe-coded slop."

— Maggie Appleton, GitHub Next

7 / 8

So what's the alternative? It's not "don't use AI." It's "use AI with taste."

Look at the left column — that's slop. Fast, cheap, copies everything including the bad parts. Each piece seems fine on its own, but together they create a mess.

The right column is craftsmanship. It's slower. It has opinions. It asks "is this actually good?" before copying it.

That quote from Maggie Appleton at GitHub is the thesis of this entire talk: craftsmanship is the differentiator.

In a world where anyone can generate content instantly, the people and products that care about quality will stand out. Not because they're using better AI — because they're applying human judgment to the AI.

THE TAKEAWAY

Stay Human

The best defense against AI slop is human taste. Ask: "Would I be proud to put my name on this?"

Don't accept slop
